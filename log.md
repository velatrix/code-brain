Lessons worth keeping outside the repo (Android automation in general):
- **Multi-touch on UiAutomator2 (server 10.2.1) needs one exact W3C-actions shape:** all fingers down in one tick → one 20 ms `pause` → one 20 ms `pointerMove` per tick → the hold before lift as a `pause` → all fingers up in one tick. A move that starts at its own finger's down instant collides into `duplicate pointer id` (HTTP 400). A multi-tick first move opens with a zero-movement MOVE, and the map never recognises the gesture.
- **Pad the resting finger.** While one finger moves, every other finger that is down must emit a zero-distance move. With a `pause` there, Android 15 rejects the MOVEs as inconsistent and the UiAutomator2 instrumentation dies, taking the session with it.
- **An Appium session freezes the app's animators.** `disableWindowAnimation` sets the animator duration scale to 0, so anything a test target animates with Android animators stops (MapLibre's location pulse did). A test target that must animate under automation has to draw off the frame clock.
- **Mock-location reset order (`io.appium.settings`):** stop the service, wait until it is gone, check that `dumpsys location` shows no `[mock]` provider, and only then deny the mock-location app-op. Deny first and the GPS provider stays a test provider holding the last mocked fix for every later app. `am stopservice` exits 255 even on success.
- **Recognition eats part of every map gesture.** A ×2 finger-span pinch zoomed MapLibre by only +0.64 at 1200 dp/s (+0.85 at 300 dp/s); about 5.5° of a turn and 11 dp of a drag go the same way. A test that needs an exact camera should reach a known view (a deep link, a re-centre) rather than count on a gesture.
- **Offsets in dp from the map view's centre are portable** across phone, tablet and both orientations: the same lat/lng to 0.4 m, because map SDKs draw the world at a fixed dp size per zoom level. Percentages of the map are not.

Three runner defects recorded in the repo backlog: **B23** a bare `id` selector matches a substring of the fully qualified resource-id, so `{ "id": "map" }` matched 26 elements in an app whose package name contains "map"; **B24** orientation and viewport are transposed on a tablet whose natural orientation is landscape; **B25** a UiAutomator2 screenshot sometimes drops the Windows emulator's adb transport, and the session with it (`adb exec-out screencap` did not fail in the same conditions). **B22** (`Swipe` from→to long-presses before moving) was confirmed on a device; MI2 fixes it. Next: MI2 (gestures).

Lessons worth keeping outside the repo (Android location mocking in general):
- **A correctly stopped mock still lingers as the last known location.** With the helper's service stopped and its test providers gone, every provider still reported the mocked fix as its last location, and a freshly launched app read it, flagged mock, until the device's own fix arrived (3.5 s on an emulator; indoors on a phone it may never come). - **Google Play services keeps a location cache of its own, which `dumpsys location` does not show** — the one `getLastLocation()` answers from. Read it with `adb shell dumpsys activity service com.google.android.gms/com.google.android.location.internal.GoogleLocationManagerService`: the `last location (fine)` and `(coarse)` lines, and a history of `flp mocked by` / `flp unmocked by`. It drops the cache only once it has itself seen location off, asynchronously: a quick off/on left the mocked fix there in 2 of 6 runs while the platform looked clean, and the next test's app read it. Verify after the toggle, and retry with location off for longer (1 s, then 3 s). Its location engine's own `location: {fused, mock, …}` line survives the toggle but is not what apps get; checking it gave false positives.
- **Undo a mock in this order:** stop the helper's service and wait until it is gone; only then deny its mock-location app-op (deny first and all three providers stay stranded as test providers); wait for Play services to record `flp unmocked`; then the location toggle. `am stopservice` exits 255 even when it worked. A stranded provider is recovered by a clean start and stop with the app-op allowed.

A settle timeout should also report the last change that broke the quiet window, not the last pair of frames: a jumping map is between jumps most of the time, so the last pair read 0 %.

Lessons worth keeping outside the repo (finding icons on a phone screen; Flutter and UiAutomator2):
- **Mask the template.** Even a tight crop of a map pin is about 40 % background. Unmasked, the pin scored 0.53–0.57 over a busy background and was not found; masked with OpenCV's GrabCut, 0.95–0.98. GrabCut is random unless seeded (`cv.setRNGSeed`).
- **GrabCut needs a margin round the object.** On a tight crop its border cuts through the icon, so the "certain background" it learns from is the icon's own colours, and half the crop came back as icon. Crop a few dp more and seed it with the object's rectangle.
- **Leave the mask's anti-aliased rim out.** The outermost pixels blend the icon with whatever was behind it when captured. On the real Google Maps app, a pin zoomed in over new ground scored 0.83 with its whole mask and 0.97 without its outer 2 px.
- **Shape alone mistakes a same-shaped icon in another colour** (0.76 against a 0.8 threshold). Check the mean colour under the mask as well: CIE76 ΔE ≤ 20 separated true matches (0–1.5) from other colours (75+).
- **Search coarse to fine, off the event loop.** A masked `matchTemplate` over five scales took 2.0–4.4 s on a phone or tablet screen. One pass on a copy shrunk until the template is 16 px, then full-resolution windows round the candidates at ×0.95, ×1 and ×1.05, took 50–90 ms with the same scores. opencv.js blocks its thread, so run it in a worker.
- **opencv.js 5.0 in Node:** `require()` returns a Promise; ready in about 0.2 s, about 150 MB more once a search has run. `Mat.roi().clone()` can still share its parent's pixels — build Mats from raw buffers. And `sharp` writes a one-channel raw image as three channels unless told `.toColourspace('b-w')`.
- **A still screen right after a tap may be about to move.** Apps answer a single tap only after the double-tap window (about 0.3–0.5 s); a map about to re-centre on a tapped marker holds still until then. Count no stillness inside that window.
- **For full-resolution frames on an emulator, use `adb exec-out screencap -p`.** It is pixel-identical to UiAutomator2's screenshot, and never dropped the Windows emulator's adb transport (0 of 33 sessions), which UiAutomator2's `GET /screenshot` sometimes does.
- **UiAutomator2's MJPEG server listens on a fixed device port, 7810.** The driver forwards each session's host `mjpegServerPort` to it; re-forwarding that host port to the same number on the device works only for the first device on a host.
- **UiAutomator2 reads a Flutter label as ""**: Flutter puts labels in `content-desc`, and `GET /element/{id}/text` returns only `text`. Fall back to `content-desc`. A Flutter semantics `identifier` becomes a plain `resource-id`, with no package prefix.
- **flutter_map 8 answers gestures unlike native map SDKs.** With its defaults the multi-finger gesture race is off, so its 20° `rotationThreshold` never applies: a turn starts once a finger passes Flutter's 18 dp touch slop, then counts from where the fingers went down. A pinch is measured from the fingers' first span, so ×2 is exactly +1 zoom at any speed. The default double-tap-drag zoom adds zoom × drag ÷ 360 of the current zoom, so 150 dp jumps about 10 levels, and it also reports its first tap as a tap. Single taps wait 250 ms; two taps are a double tap only within 250 ms and 48 dp. There is no camera-idle event: derive it from the move, rotate, fling and double-tap-zoom events, plus the fling decision that follows every gesture.
- **An inspector's first screenshot can come before the app has drawn** (map tiles, markers). Refresh before cropping an icon from it.

Lessons worth keeping outside the repo (streams over adb; black-box readiness; CI; docs):
- **A strict HTTP client turns a server's early bytes into a failed stream.** UiAutomator2's MJPEG server writes each frame to every client, including one whose request it has not read yet: it postpones that client's `HTTP/1.0 200 OK` but writes the frame anyway (unchanged upstream through 10.6.6). Node's parser then fails with `Parse Error: Expected HTTP/`. Read a multipart JPEG stream as bytes and split it on the JPEG markers — the header becomes noise to skip, like the boundaries. Ask with HTTP/1.0, so no server can answer chunked, whose size lines would land inside frames.
- **Over `adb forward`, the device side connects before your request is written.** The adb server, a process of its own, opens the device connection as soon as you connect locally; your request goes out when your event loop gets to it. A device-side server that acts on connect sees a connection with nothing in it for as long as your process is busy.
- **Reproducing an event-loop race in Node:** `net.connect` to an IP address issues its connect on `process.nextTick`, so a busy-wait placed right after `http.get()` runs before the connect has even started. Queue the stall with `process.nextTick` to land it after the connect: 40 failures in 40, against none in 600 without.
- **An app launch returns before the app has drawn.** `activateApp` and `am start` return once the activity starts; a map's tiles and markers come later. For a black-box "ready", wait until the screen stops changing — the device's consecutive frames compared, over a short quiet window: a first capture showed the drawn map 0 times in 10 without that, 10 in 10 with it. A still splash screen that outlasts the quiet window still passes for drawn, so keep a Refresh.
- **Moving a control to shared chrome changes the lifecycles under it.** Refresh used to live in one mode, and reaching it unmounted the others, which cleared their state. With Refresh in the header every mode stays mounted, so each has to clear what belonged to the old screen itself — a drawn rectangle, its matches.
- **GitHub's `reactivecircus/android-emulator-runner` runs each `script:` line as its own `sh -c`** (read from its source; its README does not say). Variables and `cd` do not carry to the next line, and the first failing line ends the step, so collect logs on the same line as the run: `run; s=$?; collect; exit $s`. Poll Appium's `/status` rather than sleeping, and redirect a background server's output, or it holds the step's pipe open.
- **A Markdown table breaks silently** when an appended row is separated from it by a blank line or a `---`: the row renders as text. Four rows of the repo's decision log had come loose that way.
- **When a UI decision is reversed, grep the docs for the old one.** A run dialog replaced by a menu on 2026-09-09 was still described on five pages two weeks later.
